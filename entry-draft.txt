CANDIDATE POOL — "One Line, One Laugh"
Judged, in order: 1) laugh on first read  2) surprise (the turn IS the joke)
3) economy  4) freshness (no stock jokes/puns)  5) clean wording read aloud.
Length for its own sake counts for nothing, so brevity is free.

TEST APPLIED TO EACH: can an attentive reader guess the last four words?
If yes, criterion 2 is dead and the line loses to anything sharper.

--- CUT: generic, stock turf, or no real turn ---
01. Smoke detector chirps at 3 a.m. — "it's not a safety device, it's a hostage
    with a schedule."  CUT: 3 a.m. battery chirp is worn-out stand-up turf.
02. Self-checkout calling a supervisor because I weighed my own banana.
    CUT: the single most entered observational topic of the decade.
03. "Per my last email" = a man in a cardigan loading a gun.
    CUT: meme-fossil. Judge will have seen it a thousand times.
04. Grandfather lived to 97 on bacon and spite.  CUT: bacon-and-cigarettes
    longevity joke with one word swapped. Freshness: zero.
05. Marriage is two people disagreeing about the thermostat forever.
    CUT: restates a familiar saying — explicitly penalised.
06. Every houseplant I own is a slow hostage negotiation I'm losing.
    CUT: nice compression, but it's an image, not a turn. No laugh beat.
07. Told my mother I was seeing someone; she asked if they were a professional.
    CUT: the "seeing someone" double meaning is a known gag shape.
08. Second monitor so I can watch two things fail at once.  CUT: guessable.
09. My dad speaks only in forwarded articles — leaving a trail in case he gets
    lost.  CUT: genuinely original, but it lands as poignant, not funny.
10. I've started lying to my fitness tracker to protect its feelings.
    CUT: too slight; charming, won't win a pair.
11. Meta line addressed to the judge.  CUT: the brief rewards lines that land,
    not lines that explain themselves — and every third entrant will try it.

--- SHORTLIST ---
12. "I asked the bartender to surprise me and the surprise was finding out
    what he thinks of me."  Turn is real but abstract; no image.
13. "My bank flagged suspicious activity on my card. It was me, buying
    vegetables."  Clean and quick, but the turn is guessable from "suspicious".
14. "Nothing on my to-do list has changed in a year, so it's not a plan
    anymore, it's a confession."  Good final word. Setup slightly familiar.
15. "I tidied for two hours before the cleaner arrived, so what I actually
    paid for was an audience."  Relatable, real reframe. Mid-strength surprise.
16. "Meditation has given me the stillness to finally win an argument from
    2014."  Specific and sharp; the intrusive-thought angle is semi-known.
17. "I cleared my calendar for a month and found out my personality was
    load-bearing."  79 chars, unguessable word, very fresh. Risk: more wry
    than loud — reads clever rather than funny.
18. "I bought a standing desk. Now I have my breakdowns at full height."
    68 chars, absurd dignity, unguessable pivot. Risk: slight.

--- WINNER ---
19. "I keep my high school yearbook like a fire extinguisher: unopened,
    within reach, in case of sudden confidence."
    Why it wins the pair: two mechanisms stacked, not one. It borrows the
    "IN CASE OF FIRE" idiom, then reverses what counts as the emergency —
    confidence is the fire. The last three words are unguessable (criterion 2),
    the image is concrete and instant (criterion 1), nothing is explained,
    and the three short beats before the turn give it rhythm aloud
    (criterion 5). Under 200 by a wide margin (criterion 3).
    Refine: wording of the three beats, and "high school" vs "my yearbook".
